Instability typology — structural × acute

Each subject placed by structural fragility (horizontal) against acute 2025–26 shock (vertical). Dashed lines are the national medians; the four quadrants sort regions into distinct policy problems. Point colour = combined score, size = combined score.

Quadrant reading

Chronic + acutely stressed (top-right) — poor to begin with and hit hard now: the ethnic republics of south Siberia and the Far East (Tuva, Altai Rep., Buryatia, Zabaykalsky, Kalmykia) where deep poverty and fiscal dependence meet the heaviest per-capita war burden.

Newly stressed (top-left) — solid structural base, sharp 2025–26 shock: the single-industry industrial regions (Kemerovo, Khakassia, Chelyabinsk, Samara) as coal, steel and autos are hit by price collapse and sanctions, and budgets buckle.

Chronic-fragile, quieter now (bottom-right) — structurally poor but currently buffered: the North Caucasus republics (Ingushetia, Dagestan, Chechnya) — federal transfers, a young population and lower per-capita mobilisation soften the acute axis.

Comparatively resilient (bottom-left) — the metropolitan and resource cores: Moscow, St Petersburg, Tatarstan, the Tyumen oil-and-gas trio.

Structural pillars

S1 Income poverty — poverty rate and wages vs the subsistence minimum. anchors: Rosstat wages 2023, RIA rank 2025

S2 Joblessness — open + informal unemployment and weak labour demand. anchors: Rosstat/CEIC unemployment 2024

S3 Demographic decline — natural decrease, out-migration, low life expectancy, ageing.

S4 Fiscal dependence — reliance on federal transfers / weak own revenue.

Acute pillars (2025–26)

A5 Budget distress — deficit vs own revenue, debt-service growth, service cuts, wage arrears. anchors: MinFin/Moscow Times/Riddle 2025–26

A6 Sectoral shock — coal/steel/auto/refining exposure and monotown density.

A7 War burden — per-capita mobilisation losses and recruitment dependence. anchors: Mediazona/BBC Feb 2026

A8 Price/fuel stress — inflation bite on real incomes + 2025–26 fuel-supply strain.

Method

How the index is built

Each of 83 federal subjects is scored 0–100 (higher = more stress) on eight indicators. The two axes are weighted composites:

structural = .30·S1 + .25·S2 + .20·S3 + .25·S4
acute = .30·A5 + .25·A6 + .25·A7 + .20·A8
combined = .5·structural + .5·acute

Map colour uses a per-layer percentile stretch (p5–p95) so each layer reads with full contrast. A subject's peak driver is the pillar furthest above the national mean (z-score), not its raw maximum — so the label names what makes a region distinct, not just what is high everywhere.

Hard anchors (unemployment, wages, per-capita war losses, named fiscal-distress regions) carry high confidence; where per-region current data is thin, scores are graded analyst judgment built on the region's known structural profile and flagged M/L.

Confidence & caveats

  • Russian official statistics systematically understate poverty and informal joblessness; treat absolute levels as floors.
  • Chechnya and, to a lesser degree, other North-Caucasus data are politically managed — scored low-confidence.
  • Record-low national unemployment (~2.3%) reflects a war-driven labour shortage, not health; it masks structural weakness, so S2 weights informality over the headline rate.
  • Large nominal deficits in rich resource regions (Moscow, Yamalo-Nenets, Khanty-Mansi) are investment/reserve-funded, not distress — A5 scores them low despite the headline.
  • Crimea & Sevastopol are occupied Ukrainian territory, scored low-confidence and excluded from the national reference distribution; the four other occupied oblasts are excluded outright.
  • This is a conditions map. It does not forecast protest, elite defection or regime change.
